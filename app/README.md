# The app

Two static pages, no server: `index.html` (the tool) and `status.html` (the public status page), both on `cashu-bundle.js` (cashu-ts 4.13 bundled with esbuild from `lib-entry.mjs`:
`npx esbuild lib-entry.mjs --bundle --format=iife --global-name=cashu --minify --outfile=cashu-bundle.js`). `index.html` also loads `qrcode.min.js` for the invoice QR (qrcode-generator 2.0.4 by Kazuhiko Arase, MIT; minified with esbuild, licence header kept), and `catid.js`, the cat recognition, which runs `catid-worker.js` on the files in `ai/1/` and `ai/2/` (121 MB, fetched only after a tap): ONNX Runtime Web 1.30.0 (MIT), YOLOS-tiny and DINOv2 small, quantised to 8 bits (Apache 2.0), and, for the cat's face (version 2, 7 Nov), AnyFace (MIT) and PetFace (its weights for non-commercial research only), quantised to 8 bits too. `ai/1/LICENSES.txt` and `ai/2/LICENSES.txt` name each file with its source, revision and SHA-256. Those files never change; a new version goes in a new folder.

`index.html` is laid out like a wallet: one thing per screen, the primary button at the bottom, a lock bar (locked → certified → released) on every token, the amount as the hero with a € estimate (rate from mempool.space, cached ten minutes), a token card instead of raw `cashuB…` for the clinic and Streetcat, a home screen for Streetcat listing the campaigns made in this browser, and one moment of motion when a payment lands. The header is the three roles — Donor, Rescue and Partner (the clinic, a shop or another); Rescue is Streetcat's role, named for any animal rescue (StreetCat, 6 Nov: «pon rescue»), while the texts inside still say Streetcat and its address stays `/streetcat` — with the one on screen in its colour (its keys' colour), and, apart, a magnifier icon for the status page (labelled Status; StreetCat, 6 Nov: «en el header, la B», then «el icono, pues la lupa»). Each role sees only its tabs; a role in the header switches to it, and the role on screen, tapped again, goes back to its first screen. On any other screen a framed ← at the header's left goes back: to the screen above it (`BACK`) when that belongs to the same role, otherwise to the role's first screen. The phone's own back does the same: every screen opened is a step in the browser's history (`history.pushState`), so back returns to the previous screen instead of leaving the page (StreetCat, 6 Nov, asking for «una flechita para volver atrás en los menús»: «haz la A»). The role is kept in the browser; a first visit opens on Donate, and a campaign link always opens Donate. The status page has the same header, Status marked, and a role there opens the app as that role. Each role also has its own address that shows only that role, with no switching: `/donate`, `/clinic`, `/streetcat` (rewrites in `vercel.json`; locally `index.html?role=clinic`), and `/status` for the status page. Both pages can be added to a phone's home screen as an app (`manifest.webmanifest`, `icons/`): the clinic gets a link to `/clinic` and installs it. Tabs:

- **Rescue: what Streetcat pays for** (7 Nov) — Home shows four kinds two by two (sterilisation, vet care, food, other), each with its pots, its requests and what is left to spend; each kind has its own screen. A campaign is a pot of one kind, for one colony or every colony, with the partner who confirms the spending (a clinic, the shop that sells the feed, or another): kind, colony, partner and what the partner is (`type`, `colony`, `partner`, `pkind`) are signed with the campaign (`core.js`), optional, and change neither its id nor its ledger. "Choose colony", "Choose partner" and "Choose pot" each open a list with "+ New". A request is paid only from a pot of its kind, for that colony or every colony; the pot brings its partner and the partner's key, and the cost takes the pot's donations, the oldest first. Partners and colonies are kept in this browser (`vetlock.sc.partners`, `vetlock.sc.colonies`). Two pots of one partner made the same day get different locks (the second refunds a second earlier), and a key that is not a point of the curve is refused (`pointOk`).
- **Partner: confirm** (7 Nov) — the header's "Partner" (inside, the role is still `clinic`). What the pot says the partner is decides its words: a clinic confirms a procedure on a cat as before; a shop a delivery ("Confirm delivery", for which colony, what), with a photo of the delivery note and of the goods, at least one; another partner what was delivered or done. The photos are made small on the phone, named by SHA-256 in the signed confirmation (`statement.photos`, slots `note` and `goods`) and sent to Streetcat by relay in parts encrypted to its key (`streetcat.answer-photo`); Rescue shows them under the request before it releases.
- **Donate** — open the campaign link, choose an amount and pay the **Lightning invoice** from any wallet (QR, "open in wallet", copy). Under Pay with Lightning, as big as it and one under another: Pay with ecash, Take my sats back (StreetCat, 6 Nov) and Check for requests (7 Nov), with no line of text under them. **Check for requests** asks the relays for the campaigns Streetcat has announced and lists the open ones where the campaign shows, one per line in its own colour, the one closing first on top; a tap opens one as its link would. Only a campaign signed with Streetcat's published key, named by the announcement and signed by its ledger key, and still open, is listed; no relay reached is said in red. The page asks the campaign's mint for the invoice (NUT-04) and, once it is paid, mints the sats directly as proofs locked to *Streetcat + clinic* (2 signatures), with a refund path, after the deadline, to a key generated in your browser — a new key for every donation. The invoice and its key are kept in the browser, so a reload (a phone switching to the wallet app) picks the payment up again. Or paste a plain ecash token from the campaign's mint and the page swaps it into the same lock. Either way you get a receipt (keep it private: it holds that key; the browser keeps a copy) and the locked token to send to Streetcat (copy or share). Closed campaigns refuse donations.
- **Clinic: certify** — the clinic's key lives in its browser; it checks the token names that key (or an older one kept there), signs offline, and hands back a *certified* token. If the token names no key held in this browser, it says which keys the token is locked to. It refuses to sign after the deadline.
- **Streetcat: release** — adds Streetcat's signature and swaps at the mint; the mint refuses unless the clinic's signature is there. Without the Streetcat key the token names, the page stops before asking the mint.
- **Donor: refund** — after the deadline, the receipt alone takes the sats back (or just the locked token, from the browser that made the donation: it lists them). Optionally paste a Lightning invoice and the refund is paid straight to it (NUT-05); the page checks the invoice with the mint first, and whatever the invoice did not ask for comes back as ecash.
- **Streetcat: new campaign** — first reads the mint (NUT-06 info, NUT-02 keysets): it needs NUT-11, NUT-07 and NUT-12, says whether NUT-04 Lightning invoices work and what the input fee is. Then builds the donation link for one operation (name, mint, both public keys, deadline, optionally where donors send the token) and the empty ledger for its status page, and announces the campaign on the relays for donors' Check for requests (kind 30078, `d` = "announce:" and the campaign's id, so it never replaces the ledger; signed with the campaign's ledger key; it expires at the deadline). If no relay takes it, it says so in red, and Home has "announce to donors".
- **Clinic: home** — its requests (to review, done), then six buttons of one size, full width, one under another: Check for requests, Open a request, Certify a token, Offline setup (what the phone needs to certify without coverage; in red while something is missing), Cat records, Settings. An empty list of cats says nothing. With two requests listed it all fits an iPhone 14–16 screen; with more, the page scrolls a little (StreetCat, 6 Nov: «botones grandes… del mismo tamaño»; «pon los botones a pantalla completa largos»).
- **Requests: the cat as caught** — making a request, Streetcat can add up to three photos of the cat (face and both sides), taken as it is caught and made small on the phone like the record's (StreetCat, 6 Nov: «para ver que realmente se está certificando el gato que es»). The request names them by SHA-256 (`photos`, optional; an older app ignores them). They follow it by relay, in parts encrypted to the clinic's key (`streetcat.request-photo`; each part names that key, so a part that comes before its request is kept), and Streetcat's request shows when they have gone ("its 3 photos sent"). The clinic sees them on the request, under "Photos from Streetcat, when caught", to check it is the right cat before confirming; a tap shows one big. In person, by QR or link, there is no room for them: the request comes first ("on their way"), and they arrive by relay once the clinic has a connection, without unticking its box. A request saved as a file carries them.
- **Requests: is it the cat?** — once the clinic's record of the cat is on Streetcat's phone (signed with the campaign's clinic key, naming the request), Streetcat's request shows the record's photos under its own, view by view ("When caught · your photos", "At the clinic, asleep · MOCHI's record"), to compare them by eye, with a link to the record. The cat recognition of "Already in the records?" adds a hint, one-sided: under a score of 0.40 it says in red "These may not be the same cat. Look at them, and at the chip, before releasing.", and so does the request's line; otherwise "nothing points to another cat", never "the same cat". The score is the mean, over the request's photos (face, sides), of each one's closest photo in the record. Calibrated on the evaluation's photos, each cat's split into a request and a record: with 2–3 photos a side, the right cat was warned of 0–5 % of the time, another cat 73–80 %, one of the same coat 51–62 %; with one photo in the record the right cat 15–19 %, so it needs two ("Too few photos in the record to compare them here"). Cats of one coat can look alike to it: two orange kittens scored higher (0.78) than one of them against its own other photos (0.69). The first time it waits for a tap (121 MB, as at the clinic); then it compares by itself, and the result is kept with the request. The test photos were of cats awake, and a record's are of a cat asleep: the line is to be checked again with the first real cats. Since version 2 (7 Nov, the face too) the score is on another scale: each pair of photos is compared by DINOv2 and by PetFace, each cosine is put on one scale (how far above the usual cosine of two different cats it is, in their spread) and the two are averaged, and the line is 1.0 on it (`CATID.WARN`); a result kept from version 1 is worked out again. On 54 of the test photos (13 cats), with 2–3 photos a side and the same false alarm for the right cat (5 %), it warns of another cat 84–89 % of the time (version 1: 66–76 %) and of one of the same coat 64–71 % (45–56 %).
- **Clinic: cat record** — with the cat still asleep, the clinic writes its record: name, colony, microchip (15 digits), sex, ear tip, coat, the procedure and its date, notes, and up to four photos (face, left side, right side, the nose up close). Each photo is made small on the phone: JPEG, at most 640 px and 38 kB, drawn anew so nothing the camera wrote in the file goes with it (place, phone, time). Opened from a request ("MOCHI's record: photos, chip"), it starts from the request's cat, procedure and date and names the request and its campaign. It is short (StreetCat, 6 Nov: one screen of an iPhone): what is known already is one line with "edit" (the request's cat, procedure and date; without a request, the procedure of the cat before, or sterilisation, and today), coat and notes are folded, and a photo tile opens the phone's camera straight away (`capture="environment"`; a computer offers its files). The record is signed with the clinic's statement key (`streetcat/cat-record/v1`, core.js `signCat`) and names each photo by its SHA-256; it goes to Streetcat by itself, encrypted: the record through the outbox like an answer, the photos (kept in IndexedDB, too big for the outbox in localStorage) in parts of at most 26 000 characters straight to the relays, each part again until two relays have it. Or "save as file": the record and its photos in one. Nothing in it is public.
- **Clinic: Check the cat** (7 Nov) — on a record made from Streetcat's request, Streetcat's photos as caught show at once under the clinic's own, and the one button compares the clinic's photos with both: with the request (the hint of "Requests: is it the cat?", one-sided, before signing: "These may not be the cat of Streetcat's request", in red) and with the records, as below.
- **Clinic: Already in the records?** — once the new record has a photo of the face or a side, and the clinic has records with such photos, the new photos are compared on the phone with theirs (StreetCat, 6 Nov: «que les avise de posibles redundancias»). The three records that look most like it are shown in order, each with its photo most like the new one, its name, colony and date; a tap opens one, and ← comes back to the new record as it was. It is only an order, never "the same cat". In the evaluation (58 photos of 14 named cats from Wikimedia Commons, kept out of the repository), the cat's own record came first 66 % of the time and among the first three 97 %, but no score told a cat already recorded from a new one, so the app shows the order and nothing more: "Only an order, not a match: open one to compare the chip". How it works: YOLOS-tiny finds the cat and the photo is cut to it (10 % margin, a padded square); DINOv2 small turns it into 384 numbers; a record's score is the mean, over the new photos, of each one's closest photo in the record (cosine). The photos are resized as Pillow does it, so every browser gives the same numbers. Since version 2 (7 Nov) the face counts too: AnyFace finds the cat's face and five points on it (eyes, nose, mouth corners), the face is turned and scaled onto PetFace's cat template, and PetFace turns it into 512 numbers; a face counts only from a score of 0.6, and with no face in either photo DINOv2 decides alone. On the test photos the right cat came first 89 % of the time, against 63 % with DINOv2 alone. The first time, it asks before the 121 MB come, then shows them coming; they come from the app's own site, and the service worker keeps them, apart from the app (`streetcat-ai-1`), for use without coverage. A donor's phone never downloads them. Each photo's numbers are worked out once and kept in IndexedDB (`streetcat-catid`), under its SHA-256. Nothing leaves the phone. If the recognition cannot come, it says so in red, with the button to try again. Offline setup has a line for it (StreetCat, 6 Nov: «añade offline setup»): ⚠ until it is saved, with a button to get it ahead of time, on wifi, showing the megabytes come. It saves the files into the service worker's copy without starting the recognition, and it is not needed to be "Ready for offline use".
- **Requests: sent by themselves** (7 Nov) — the request, the clinic's answer, the records and the photos go by relay as soon as they are made (the outbox sends them again until two relays have them), and each says so: "✓ sent · 2 of 3 relays have it", then "received by the clinic" or "✓ received by Streetcat" when the other phone's receipt comes back. The link, file and QR buttons show only when it has not gone (no connection, the browser cannot reach relays, or every relay refused it), under a red "✗ Not sent…" (StreetCat: «solo salgan en el modo offline… si no es redundante»); once it has gone, a small link ("Clinic offline? Show it as QR", "Streetcat offline? Show it as QR") still shows it as a QR for the other phone in person.
- **Cats** — the clinic's records, with whether each has gone, and Streetcat's, as they arrive from the relays (or from a file): searchable by name, chip digits, colony or coat, each with its photos and what the clinic wrote. Streetcat takes a record by relay only from the clinic key of one of its campaigns, keeps a photo only when its SHA-256 is the one the record names, and lists a record signed by any other clinic key (from a file) as "unknown clinic", in red.
- **Hold to confirm** (7 Nov) — every button that moves money or signs (Take my sats back, Lock my donation, Certify, Confirm procedure, Send the report, Sign and send to Streetcat, Sign and release, Release, Make the request, Make the campaign, Accept and sign) needs holding for two seconds: it fills from the left in the colour of whose screen it is (donor orange, Rescue green, clinic blue), blinks twice, buzzes where the phone allows it, and acts (StreetCat: «que la barra hubiese que pulsarla… hasta que se cargase entera… y luego… algún parpadeo»). A tap alone does nothing and says "hold to confirm"; let go early and the fill empties. Space or Enter held does the same; a long press opens no menu. Pay with Lightning only shows an invoice (the wallet is where it is paid), so it stays a tap. `vetlock.holdms` in a browser's storage sets the time (the tests set 0: a tap).
- **Keys** — generate the browser keys; **Export all** / **Import all** move them, with everything the browser keeps (campaigns, donations, requests, partners, colonies, photos), to another browser: what that one has stays. Everywhere a key is shown it is painted in the colour of its role — orange (Bitcoin's) for donors' refund keys, blue for the clinic, green for Streetcat — with each group of 4 characters in its own shade taken from the key itself, so the same key always looks the same and two keys of one role can be told apart at a glance. A new or imported clinic or Streetcat key never deletes the one before: it is kept in the browser and still signs the tokens locked to it, and exports carry it. Keys are per browser and per address: another browser, a private tab or an app's built-in browser keeps its own.

Every call that creates ecash (minting a paid invoice, and the swaps that lock pasted ecash, release and refund) builds its outputs in the page and saves them in the browser *before* asking the mint. If the answer is lost, the page asks the mint for it again (NUT-09 restore): at once, or the next time it opens. Released tokens are kept in the browser too, until Streetcat moves them to its wallet. A campaign link is checked before any money moves: a whole-number deadline in the next two years, valid and distinct keys, an https mint; after minting or swapping, the page checks the lock written in the new proofs. `STREETCAT_KEYS` (empty for now) pins Streetcat's published key once it exists.

## Status page

`status.html?l=<ledger URL>` (or paste the ledger). A ledger is one JSON file per campaign, published by Streetcat in `app/campaigns/` — for example `app/campaigns/mochi.json`, opened as `status.html?l=campaigns/mochi.json`. Only `app/` is published, so the ledgers live inside it:

```json
{
  "campaign": { "name": "MOCHI", "op": "sterilisation", "mint": "https://mint.example", "streetcat": "02…", "clinic": "03…", "deadline": 1796083199 },
  "clinicKeySource": "https://clinic.example/where-it-published-its-key",
  "donations": ["cashuB…", "cashuB…"]
}
```

`donations` holds every locked token exactly as donors sent it. Publishing them is safe: they need the clinic's signature, or after the deadline the donor's own refund key, and each donation has its own refund key.

For every donation the page checks, in the browser and against the mint only:

1. **The lock** written in each proof: Streetcat + clinic, 2 signatures, the campaign deadline, a refund key.
2. **That the mint issued it**: the DLEQ proof (NUT-12) against the mint's published keys. `UNSPENT` alone proves nothing — a mint says that about anything it has never seen.
3. **What happened**: the proof state (NUT-07) and, for spent proofs, the witness the mint accepted. *Locked* / *refundable* (deadline passed, unspent) / *released* (clinic and Streetcat signed) / *refunded* (the donor's refund key signed).

Opened in the browser that made donations (same address as the donation app), the page also lists **the donations made from this browser**, from the app's own copies of the locked tokens, with the same checks against the mint; the campaign is read from the lock in each token (Streetcat's key first, as the app writes it). "Copy them as ledgers" gives one ledger per campaign, ready for `app/campaigns/`; refund keys are never in it.

Tokens that fail 1 or 2 are listed as *not counted*, with the reason. A box lets any donor check that their own token is listed. What the page cannot prove: that the list is complete (an omitted donation stays locked and refundable), or that the mint still holds the bitcoin behind its ecash.

Open both pages locally with any static server (`python3 -m http.server 8080 --directory app`) — a `file://` URL also works for `index.html`, since the bundle is a classic script; `status.html` needs a server to fetch a ledger by URL, but a pasted ledger works anywhere.
