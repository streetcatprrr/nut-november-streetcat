# The app

Two static pages, no server: `index.html` (the tool) and `status.html` (the public status page), both on `cashu-bundle.js` (cashu-ts 4.13 bundled with esbuild from `lib-entry.mjs`:
`npx esbuild lib-entry.mjs --bundle --format=iife --global-name=cashu --minify --outfile=cashu-bundle.js`). `index.html` also loads `qrcode.min.js` for the invoice QR (qrcode-generator 2.0.4 by Kazuhiko Arase, MIT; minified with esbuild, licence header kept), and `catid.js`, the cat recognition, which runs `catid-worker.js` on the files in `ai/1/` (48 MB, fetched only after a tap): ONNX Runtime Web 1.30.0 (MIT), and YOLOS-tiny and DINOv2 small, quantised to 8 bits (Apache 2.0). `ai/1/LICENSES.txt` names each file with its source, revision and SHA-256. Those files never change; a new version goes in `ai/2/`.

`index.html` is laid out like a wallet: one thing per screen, the primary button at the bottom, a lock bar (locked → certified → released) on every token, the amount as the hero with a € estimate (rate from mempool.space, cached ten minutes), a token card instead of raw `cashuB…` for the clinic and Streetcat, a home screen for Streetcat listing the campaigns made in this browser, and one moment of motion when a payment lands. The header is the three roles — Donor, Rescue and Clinic; Rescue is Streetcat's role, named for any animal rescue (StreetCat, 6 Nov: «pon rescue»), while the texts inside still say Streetcat and its address stays `/streetcat` — with the one on screen in its colour (its keys' colour), and, apart, a magnifier icon for the status page (labelled Status; StreetCat, 6 Nov: «en el header, la B», then «el icono, pues la lupa»). Each role sees only its tabs; a role in the header switches to it, and the role on screen, tapped again, goes back to its first screen. On any other screen a framed ← at the header's left goes back: to the screen above it (`BACK`) when that belongs to the same role, otherwise to the role's first screen. The phone's own back does the same: every screen opened is a step in the browser's history (`history.pushState`), so back returns to the previous screen instead of leaving the page (StreetCat, 6 Nov, asking for «una flechita para volver atrás en los menús»: «haz la A»). The role is kept in the browser; a first visit opens on Donate, and a campaign link always opens Donate. The status page has the same header, Status marked, and a role there opens the app as that role. Each role also has its own address that shows only that role, with no switching: `/donate`, `/clinic`, `/streetcat` (rewrites in `vercel.json`; locally `index.html?role=clinic`), and `/status` for the status page. Both pages can be added to a phone's home screen as an app (`manifest.webmanifest`, `icons/`): the clinic gets a link to `/clinic` and installs it. Tabs:

- **Donate** — open the campaign link, choose an amount and pay the **Lightning invoice** from any wallet (QR, "open in wallet", copy). Under Pay with Lightning, as big as it and one under another: Pay with ecash and Take my sats back (StreetCat, 6 Nov), with no line of text under them. The page asks the campaign's mint for the invoice (NUT-04) and, once it is paid, mints the sats directly as proofs locked to *Streetcat + clinic* (2 signatures), with a refund path, after the deadline, to a key generated in your browser — a new key for every donation. The invoice and its key are kept in the browser, so a reload (a phone switching to the wallet app) picks the payment up again. Or paste a plain ecash token from the campaign's mint and the page swaps it into the same lock. Either way you get a receipt (keep it private: it holds that key; the browser keeps a copy) and the locked token to send to Streetcat (copy or share). Closed campaigns refuse donations.
- **Clinic: certify** — the clinic's key lives in its browser; it checks the token names that key (or an older one kept there), signs offline, and hands back a *certified* token. If the token names no key held in this browser, it says which keys the token is locked to. It refuses to sign after the deadline.
- **Streetcat: release** — adds Streetcat's signature and swaps at the mint; the mint refuses unless the clinic's signature is there. Without the Streetcat key the token names, the page stops before asking the mint.
- **Donor: refund** — after the deadline, the receipt alone takes the sats back (or just the locked token, from the browser that made the donation: it lists them). Optionally paste a Lightning invoice and the refund is paid straight to it (NUT-05); the page checks the invoice with the mint first, and whatever the invoice did not ask for comes back as ecash.
- **Streetcat: new campaign** — first reads the mint (NUT-06 info, NUT-02 keysets): it needs NUT-11, NUT-07 and NUT-12, says whether NUT-04 Lightning invoices work and what the input fee is. Then builds the donation link for one operation (name, mint, both public keys, deadline, optionally where donors send the token) and the empty ledger for its status page.
- **Clinic: home** — its requests (to review, done), then six buttons of one size, full width, one under another: Check for requests, Open a request, Certify a token, Offline setup (what the phone needs to certify without coverage; in red while something is missing), Cat records, Settings. An empty list of cats says nothing. With two requests listed it all fits an iPhone 14–16 screen; with more, the page scrolls a little (StreetCat, 6 Nov: «botones grandes… del mismo tamaño»; «pon los botones a pantalla completa largos»).
- **Requests: the cat as caught** — making a request, Streetcat can add up to three photos of the cat (face and both sides), taken as it is caught and made small on the phone like the record's (StreetCat, 6 Nov: «para ver que realmente se está certificando el gato que es»). The request names them by SHA-256 (`photos`, optional; an older app ignores them). They follow it by relay, in parts encrypted to the clinic's key (`streetcat.request-photo`; each part names that key, so a part that comes before its request is kept), and Streetcat's request shows when they have gone ("its 3 photos sent"). The clinic sees them on the request, under "Photos from Streetcat, when caught", to check it is the right cat before confirming; a tap shows one big. In person, by QR or link, there is no room for them: the request comes first ("on their way"), and they arrive by relay once the clinic has a connection, without unticking its box. A request saved as a file carries them.
- **Clinic: cat record** — with the cat still asleep, the clinic writes its record: name, colony, microchip (15 digits), sex, ear tip, coat, the procedure and its date, notes, and up to four photos (face, left side, right side, the nose up close). Each photo is made small on the phone: JPEG, at most 640 px and 38 kB, drawn anew so nothing the camera wrote in the file goes with it (place, phone, time). Opened from a request ("MOCHI's record: photos, chip"), it starts from the request's cat, procedure and date and names the request and its campaign. It is short (StreetCat, 6 Nov: one screen of an iPhone): what is known already is one line with "edit" (the request's cat, procedure and date; without a request, the procedure of the cat before, or sterilisation, and today), coat and notes are folded, and a photo tile opens the phone's camera straight away (`capture="environment"`; a computer offers its files). The record is signed with the clinic's statement key (`streetcat/cat-record/v1`, core.js `signCat`) and names each photo by its SHA-256; it goes to Streetcat by itself, encrypted: the record through the outbox like an answer, the photos (kept in IndexedDB, too big for the outbox in localStorage) in parts of at most 26 000 characters straight to the relays, each part again until two relays have it. Or "save as file": the record and its photos in one. Nothing in it is public.
- **Clinic: Already in the records?** — once the new record has a photo of the face or a side, and the clinic has records with such photos, the new photos are compared on the phone with theirs (StreetCat, 6 Nov: «que les avise de posibles redundancias»). The three records that look most like it are shown in order, each with its photo most like the new one, its name, colony and date; a tap opens one, and ← comes back to the new record as it was. It is only an order, never "the same cat". In the evaluation (58 photos of 14 named cats from Wikimedia Commons, kept out of the repository), the cat's own record came first 66 % of the time and among the first three 97 %, but no score told a cat already recorded from a new one, so the app shows the order and nothing more: "Only an order, not a match: open one to compare the chip". How it works: YOLOS-tiny finds the cat and the photo is cut to it (10 % margin, a padded square); DINOv2 small turns it into 384 numbers; a record's score is the mean, over the new photos, of each one's closest photo in the record (cosine). The photos are resized as Pillow does it, so every browser gives the same numbers. The first time, it asks before the 48 MB come, then shows them coming; they come from the app's own site, and the service worker keeps them, apart from the app (`streetcat-ai-1`), for use without coverage. A donor's phone never downloads them. Each photo's numbers are worked out once and kept in IndexedDB (`streetcat-catid`), under its SHA-256. Nothing leaves the phone. If the recognition cannot come, it says so in red, with the button to try again. Offline setup has a line for it (StreetCat, 6 Nov: «añade offline setup»): ⚠ until it is saved, with a button to get it ahead of time, on wifi, showing the megabytes come. It saves the files into the service worker's copy without starting the recognition, and it is not needed to be "Ready for offline use".
- **Cats** — the clinic's records, with whether each has gone, and Streetcat's, as they arrive from the relays (or from a file): searchable by name, chip digits, colony or coat, each with its photos and what the clinic wrote. Streetcat takes a record by relay only from the clinic key of one of its campaigns, keeps a photo only when its SHA-256 is the one the record names, and lists a record signed by any other clinic key (from a file) as "unknown clinic", in red.
- **Keys** — generate, export and import the browser keys. Everywhere a key is shown it is painted in the colour of its role — orange (Bitcoin's) for donors' refund keys, blue for the clinic, green for Streetcat — with each group of 4 characters in its own shade taken from the key itself, so the same key always looks the same and two keys of one role can be told apart at a glance. A new or imported clinic or Streetcat key never deletes the one before: it is kept in the browser and still signs the tokens locked to it, and exports carry it. Keys are per browser and per address: another browser, a private tab or an app's built-in browser keeps its own.

Every call that creates ecash (minting a paid invoice, and the swaps that lock pasted ecash, release and refund) builds its outputs in the page and saves them in the browser *before* asking the mint. If the answer is lost, the page asks the mint for it again (NUT-09 restore): at once, or the next time it opens. Released tokens are kept in the browser too, until Streetcat moves them to its wallet. A campaign link is checked before any money moves: a whole-number deadline in the next two years, valid and distinct keys, an https mint; after minting or swapping, the page checks the lock written in the new proofs. `STREETCAT_KEYS` (empty for now) pins Streetcat's published key once it exists.

## Status page

`status.html?l=<ledger URL>` (or paste the ledger). A ledger is one JSON file per campaign, published by Streetcat in `app/campaigns/` — for example `app/campaigns/mochi.json`, opened as `status.html?l=campaigns/mochi.json`. Only `app/` is published, so the ledgers live inside it:

```json
{
  "campaign": { "name": "MOCHI", "op": "sterilisation", "mint": "https://mint.example", "streetcat": "02…", "clinic": "03…", "deadline": 1796083199 },
  "clinicKeySource": "https://clinic.example/where-it-published-its-key",
  "donations": ["cashuB…", "cashuB…"]
}
```

`donations` holds every locked token exactly as donors sent it. Publishing them is safe: they need the clinic's signature, or after the deadline the donor's own refund key, and each donation has its own refund key.

For every donation the page checks, in the browser and against the mint only:

1. **The lock** written in each proof: Streetcat + clinic, 2 signatures, the campaign deadline, a refund key.
2. **That the mint issued it**: the DLEQ proof (NUT-12) against the mint's published keys. `UNSPENT` alone proves nothing — a mint says that about anything it has never seen.
3. **What happened**: the proof state (NUT-07) and, for spent proofs, the witness the mint accepted. *Locked* / *refundable* (deadline passed, unspent) / *released* (clinic and Streetcat signed) / *refunded* (the donor's refund key signed).

Opened in the browser that made donations (same address as the donation app), the page also lists **the donations made from this browser**, from the app's own copies of the locked tokens, with the same checks against the mint; the campaign is read from the lock in each token (Streetcat's key first, as the app writes it). "Copy them as ledgers" gives one ledger per campaign, ready for `app/campaigns/`; refund keys are never in it.

Tokens that fail 1 or 2 are listed as *not counted*, with the reason. A box lets any donor check that their own token is listed. What the page cannot prove: that the list is complete (an omitted donation stays locked and refundable), or that the mint still holds the bitcoin behind its ecash.

Open both pages locally with any static server (`python3 -m http.server 8080 --directory app`) — a `file://` URL also works for `index.html`, since the bundle is a classic script; `status.html` needs a server to fetch a ledger by URL, but a pasted ledger works anywhere.
