# Vet-certified donations in ecash

**Streetcat's project for Nut November 2026.** Donations in Cashu ecash that nobody can spend — not even the charity — until the veterinary clinic certifies that the operation happened. If it never happens, the donor can take the sats back after the deadline.

> *Status (4 Nov 2026): the whole flow and the public status page work in a real browser against a local Nutshell mint — see [`app/`](app/) and [`tests/`](tests/). With real sats on the Minibits mint, a donation over Lightning has been locked, certified by the clinic key and released with Streetcat's. Next: a real refund, and the first cat.*

## Why

[Streetcat](https://streetcat.org) is a registered non-profit in Spain that feeds feral colonies, pays vet bills and runs trap-neuter-return. A sterilisation costs 40–80 €; a street cat eats 70–100 g of food a day. We publish receipts for everything, but a receipt is published *after* the money moved. Cashu lets us turn that around: **the money cannot move until the receipt exists.**

## How it works

Everything uses spending conditions that already exist in the protocol ([NUT-11](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/blob/main/11.md) P2PK, [NUT-14](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/blob/main/14.md) HTLC). No mint has to change anything.

1. **Donate.** The donor pays a **Lightning invoice from any wallet** — no Cashu wallet needed — and the page (client-side, using cashu-ts) mints the ecash straight into locked proofs ([NUT-04](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/blob/main/04.md) + NUT-11): there is never a moment when the donation is spendable by anyone. The donation link is signed with Streetcat's key, so a link someone changed (another clinic key, another deadline) is refused before any money moves, and so is a link with no signature (anyone could take it off). Donors who already hold ecash on the campaign's mint can paste it instead, and the page swaps it into the same lock:
   - spendable only with **two signatures: Streetcat + the clinic** (`n_sigs = 2`), and
   - after a deadline (`locktime`), **also** spendable by the **donor's refund key** alone (a new key for every donation).
   The donor keeps the locked token, and the page sends it to Streetcat's campaign by itself (Nostr relay, end-to-end encrypted). Nobody can spend it yet. If the phone switches to the wallet app and the page reloads, the paid invoice is picked up again on return.
2. **Operate.** The cat is sterilised. Streetcat pays the clinic **in euros**, as always. The clinic's role is to attest, not to receive sats.
3. **Certify.** Streetcat sends the clinic a request — which cat, which procedure, and how much of which donations pays for it — by Nostr relay (end-to-end encrypted), or as a link, a file or a QR. The clinic reviews each request on its own and confirms it with a key it published once (on its own website or printed on its invoices), or reports a problem. Its answer signs exactly the proofs in the request, plus a declaration that names them.
4. **Release.** Streetcat checks every signature and the declaration, adds its own, and the mint accepts the swap. The sats go to Streetcat's reserve; what a split donation does not need goes back under the same lock, still the donor's to take back after the deadline. Anyone can check that the released token carried the clinic's signature.
5. **Or refund.** No certification before the deadline → the donor takes the sats back with the refund key, as ecash or paid straight to a Lightning invoice or address. The key travels in the donor's private receipt: a picture of the donation that carries its data inside (never drawn), checked once saved, optionally protected with a password. Whatever part of a donation was not released (the rest of a split one too) can be taken back. Under NUT-11 the two-signature path stays open after the deadline too (we checked Nutshell 0.22.1 and CDK 0.17.7, and a local test confirms it), so a late certification could still win the race; the clinic's screen warns when the refund date has passed, and whatever the mint settles first counts.
6. **Follow it.** The campaign's [status page](app/status.html) — its ledger published by Streetcat on Nostr, or as a file — lists every locked donation and asks the mint what happened to each one: still locked, released with the clinic's signature, or refunded. It trusts nobody, Streetcat included: the lock is read from the token, the mint's DLEQ proof (NUT-12) shows the ecash is real, and the signatures the mint returns for spent ecash (NUT-07) show who signed.

Variant for clinics that do not want a key: the clinic generates a secret code before the operation and hands over only its hash; donations are locked to that hash (HTLC) plus Streetcat's signature; the code is printed on the invoice when the operation is done.

### What it guarantees, and what it does not

- Guaranteed by the mint: the sats can only move with the clinic's signature (plus Streetcat's), or back to the donor once the deadline has passed.
- Not guaranteed: that the clinic signs honestly. That is the same trust as today's invoice — but now the clinic, not the charity, is the one who vouches.
- How far a certification reaches: the clinic signs whole proofs, and its NUT-11 signature (SIG_INPUTS) covers each proof's secret, not where its value goes. So what one certification can release is at most the value of the proofs it signs, never more; the honest app releases exactly the amount asked and puts the rest of the one proof a release splits back under the same lock, and anyone can check afterwards that it did (the rest must be at the mint under blinded messages derived from the proof). A changed Rescue app could keep that rest instead; donations are cut small (no proof over a tenth of the donation), so it is under a tenth, and most releases split nothing. What the clinic signs is what it was shown: before it can confirm, its app checks every proof's amount against the mint's own signature (the DLEQ proof of NUT-12, which every request now carries), so a request cannot say a 1 024-sat proof is worth 64. That check trusts the mint the campaign names.
- Keys: the clinic's declarations, the relay messages and the ledger are signed with keys derived from the campaign keys (k + t, with t public), so a campaign needs no new keys announced. They keep apart what each signs, not who could spend: whoever learns a derived private key learns the spending key too. So none of them ever leaves the app: worked out when needed, never exported, shown or logged.
- Trust in the mint: it holds the bitcoin and enforces the lock. It cannot forge the clinic's signature, but it could lose or keep the sats, and nothing here proves its reserves. Fine for small donations held for a few weeks; not for large sums.
- Honest wording for donors: *your sats do not pay the vet bill; we pay it in euros. Your sats are released to Streetcat only when the clinic certifies the operation, and you get them back if it never happens.*

## Prototype

`prototype/vetlock_proto.py` starts a local [Nutshell](https://github.com/cashubtc/nutshell) mint with a fake Lightning backend (no real money), creates three wallets (donor, Streetcat, clinic) and runs the whole flow. Verified output in `prototype/run-2026-11-04.log`:

| Scenario | Mint says |
|---|---|
| Streetcat tries to spend without the clinic's code | `no HTLC preimage provided` |
| Streetcat with a wrong code + signature | `HTLC preimage does not match` |
| Streetcat with the clinic's code + signature | **accepted** |
| Donor refund before the deadline | refused |
| Donor refund after the deadline (3 s lock) | **accepted** |
| Two-signature lock, Streetcat alone | `signatures (1) present for n_sigs (2)` |
| Two-signature lock, Streetcat + clinic | **accepted** |

Run it: `pip install cashu && python3 prototype/vetlock_proto.py` (Python ≥ 3.10).

### Wallet and mint support (from the [NUTs compatibility table](https://cashubtc.github.io/nuts/))

| | P2PK (NUT-11) | HTLC (NUT-14) |
|---|---|---|
| Nutshell, cdk, cashu-ts | yes | yes |
| Minibits, Cashu.me, Sovran | yes | no |
| Macadamia | no | no |
| Mints: Nutshell, cdk-mintd, nutmix | yes | yes |

Donors never need special wallet support: any Lightning wallet pays the invoice (or they send plain ecash), and the donation page does the locking; releases and refunds also go through the project's pages.
